Connect a Casio PX-330BK
  1. Turn the piano off. Connect its square USB-B port to your computer with a USB data cable. Use a USB-C adapter or USB-C to USB-B data cable if needed.
  2. Turn the piano on, then open this page in desktop Chrome or Edge.
  3. Choose Connect MIDI piano, allow access, then select CASIO USB-MIDI (or the device name your computer shows).
  4. Keep the piano in normal playing mode, not USB storage mode. Use one plain piano tone with split, layer, and auto accompaniment off. Leave Local Control on to hear the piano itself.

MIDI carries key events, not microphone audio. This page receives notes only and does not send sound or settings to your piano. In wait mode, keep sustained keys held, release notes that end, and replay repeated notes. Pedal messages do not substitute for held keys. Wait mode checks pitches only. Timed mode checks note starts and pitches with an adjustable tolerance. Hold lengths, pedal, and fingering are not graded. Balanced allows a quarter-beat timing margin, bounded to 90–300 ms.

If access fails in the Codex browser, open the site directly in Chrome or Edge. iPad Safari cannot receive Web MIDI directly. The iPad can show the score and play reference audio, but opening this site on both devices does not synchronize them. Use desktop Chrome or Edge for MIDI checking. Headphones plugged into the piano work independently of MIDI; the website count-in plays on the computer, with a visible count-in on screen. Bluetooth headphones connected to an iPad can play website reference audio, but do not automatically receive the Casio sound. A USB MIDI interface connected to the piano's MIDI OUT is another input option.

Casio manual, E-51 to E-52 · Browser MIDI support. Physical PX-330 connection has not been tested here.

OPTIONAL · SINGLE NOTES

Let the mic listen.

For left-hand notes, one at a time. It checks pitch and octave, then waits for your next note. Timing, fingering, and right-hand chords are self-checked.

Microphone off-

Put your phone near the piano’s speaker. Use a clean piano sound and no sustain pedal for this check.

Microphone audio stays on your device and is not recorded or uploaded. Experimental: bass harmonics and room noise can confuse pitch detection. Stop and use the guide if it disagrees with a known correct key.

BUILD A RELIABLE PERFORMANCE

Return, then refine.

  1. Session 1 · learn the shapes

    Try three 12-minute sessions, with breaks. Rotate left and right hands across different sections. Finish with one slow both-hands attempt.

  2. Next session · join the hands

    Play yesterday’s sections from the score before listening. Use Mix & combine; revisit the two weakest transitions. Raise tempo by 5 only after two clean returns.

  3. Before performing · warm up

    Warm up for 10 minutes. Test a few starts away from the beginning, then one full run at a reliable tempo. Keep going after a small slip.

80 BPM is an adjustable Andante reference, not a teacher-specified pass mark. A steady slower performance beats a rushed one.

Other apps, method, and compatibility

Piano Marvel is an established alternative with custom music uploads. Its microphone assessment is on iPhone/iPad; MIDI is more accurate, especially for chords. Custom uploads require MusicXML and MIDI preparation; check its current plan before subscribing.

This page uses short orientation followed by interleaved returns and active attempts with the score, informed by Carter & Grahn’s music-practice study. Effects vary; actual playing and later returns are the test.

Use a current Safari or Chrome browser. Audio starts after a tap. Microphone needs permission and HTTPS. Desktop UI and synthetic pitch detection are tested; physical phone speakers, your piano, and real microphone accuracy are NOT TESTED. No account, analytics, or audio uploads. Progress is saved only in this browser; it does not sync across devices.

Playback uses recordings of a Yamaha C5 grand piano. It preserves note timing but does not model the printed ritardando or expressive dynamics; use those markings when polishing. The final low E has no new note after it.
